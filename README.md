### Citizens Advice Gateshead – Advice in Community Project (2025)

### Project Overview
This project analyses service activity and financial outcomes from the Citizens Advice Gateshead Advice in Community Project (2025). The objective was to evaluate client demand, understand key advice issues, assess financial outcomes delivered to residents, and investigate regional variations in financial hardship using statistical analysis.The project was completed as part of a Data Analyst assessment, demonstrating data cleaning, statistical analysis, reporting, data visualisation, and evidence-based recommendation skills using R.

### Business Problem
Citizens Advice Gateshead provides advice and support services to residents experiencing financial and social challenges.

## The organisation wanted to understand:

- Service demand across the community
- The most common client issues
- Financial outcomes achieved through advice interventions
- Whether financial hardship differed across regions
- How data could support targeted service delivery and resource allocation

### Project Objectives

## Task 1 – Service Activity & Financial Outcomes

- Analyse overall client demand
- Identify the most common issue categories
- Evaluate financial outcomes generated for residents
- Produce stakeholder-focused insights and recommendations
  
## Task 2 – Regional Variations in Financial Hardship

# Investigate:
- Does average outstanding debt differ by region?
- Does the proportion of customers with debt-related issues differ by region?
- Does region still influence debt levels after accounting for income?

### Dataset

The project utilised Citizens Advice service data containing:

The analysis was conducted using anonymised and assessment-provided datasets.

### Task 2 – Regional Variations in Financial Hardship

# Research Question 1

1. Does average outstanding debt differ by region?

## Method

- Analysis of Variance (ANOVA)
  
## Result 

Average debt levels varied significantly across regions.

## Highest average debt:

- South Region
- West Region
  
## Lower average debt:

- East Region
- North Region
  
## Conclusion

Regional differences in debt levels were statistically significant.

# Research Question 2

2. Do debt-related issues differ by region?
   
## Method

- Chi-Square Test of Independence
  
## Result

A statistically significant relationship existed between:

- Region
- Debt-related issues
  
## Conclusion

# Research Question 3

3. Does region still influence debt levels after accounting for income?
   
## Method

Multiple Linear Regression

## Result

- Income was a significant predictor of debt.
- Regional differences remained significant after controlling for income.
  
## Conclusion

- Income alone does not fully explain regional variations in financial hardship.
  
## Additional factors may include:

- Housing costs
- Financial vulnerability
- Service accessibility
- Local socioeconomic conditions
- Debt-related demand was not evenly distributed across regions.

### Recommendations

Based on the analysis, Citizens Advice Gateshead could consider:

## Service Delivery

- Prioritising debt advice services in higher-debt regions
- Maintaining support for benefits and financial inclusion services
- Monitoring regional debt trends regularly
  
## Resource Allocation

- Using regional analysis to target resources more effectively
- Identifying areas experiencing the greatest financial hardship
- Supporting evidence-based planning and decision making
  
## Future Analysis

- Explore demographic patterns including age and gender
- Incorporate local deprivation indicators
- Monitor changes in demand over time
